About Retro TV

Watch TV What’s On

Retro TV is a television set that behaves like broadcast television used to. There is no library to browse and nothing to queue up. You turn it on, and whatever is scheduled is already halfway through.

Everyone is watching the same thing

Every channel runs to a fixed schedule derived from the current date and the channel’s name. Your browser works out what should be playing and seeks to that exact point in the file, so two people opening the site on opposite sides of the world see the same frame at the same moment. Turn a channel off and come back later and the programme will have moved on without you, exactly as it would have done in 1994.

The schedule is generated per UTC day rather than per local day, so nobody gets a different line-up for being in a different time zone. A programme that runs past midnight is allowed to finish, and the next day picks up where it left off.

Where the programmes come from

Everything you see is streamed directly from the Internet Archive, a non-profit library of millions of free books, films, software and web pages. This project stores no video of its own; it only keeps a list of identifiers and runtimes and points your browser at archive.org.

The Internet Archive makes all of this possible, and it is worth supporting. If you enjoy this, consider donating to them rather than to anyone else.

On rights: the programmes here are drawn from Internet Archive collections that generally hold public domain and openly licensed material, but the rights status of any individual item is between you and the Archive. This project makes no determination about the copyright status of anything it links to. Every programme in the info bar, the guide and the What’s On page links to its archive.org page — check there for the rights information on anything you care about.

Suggest a channel

The line-up is defined in a single configuration file, and adding a channel is mostly a matter of naming an Archive collection and a sensible runtime range. If there is a corner of the Archive you think deserves its own channel, say so.

Keyboard controls

  • Up and Down arrows, or the mouse wheel, change channel.
  • Digits tune directly; the set waits a moment, or press Enter.
  • Backspace or L returns to the previous channel.
  • I shows what is on.
  • Left and Right arrows, or + and −, set the volume. M mutes.
  • G opens the guide, S the picture settings.
  • ? shows this list on the television itself, and Escape closes anything that is open.

Accessibility

Every control is reachable by keyboard and carries a label. If your system asks for reduced motion, the flicker, the channel-change static and the guide’s automatic scrolling all switch off, and the picture effects can be turned off individually in the picture settings.

Retro TV is an original design. It uses no broadcaster names, logos or character art, and is not affiliated with the Internet Archive or with any television network, past or present.
